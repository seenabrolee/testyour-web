Illumi's Twisted Love? No, It's the Entire Zoldyck Household

Viewers often single out Illumi as an unhinged stalker brother only because the field scenes follow him. Inside the Zoldyck family walls, Killua is the one who looks broken—and Illumi is simply the eldest son enforcing what he was taught without question.

Author: TESTYOUR Creator Published: November 27, 2026 Reading Time: ~5 min
"It's not just Illumi's twisted love—it's the entire Zoldyck family's problem. From the outside, Illumi looks insane and Killua looks normal. From inside the Zoldyck house, Killua is the crazy one." — TESTYOUR Creator's Character Note

Whenever Hunter x Hunter fans talk about Illumi Zoldyck, the conversation usually reduces him to a creepy yandere brother with a personal obsession over Killua. Showing up disguised at the Hunter Exam, embedding a Nen needle in his brother's skull, and hunting him across the Election arc certainly makes him look like the lone villain of the family.

Step back for a second, though, and that reading misses what the Zoldyck family actually is. None of what sits inside Illumi's head was invented by Illumi alone.

1. Illumi Looks Singularly Unhinged Only Because He Gets the Screen Time

Strip away the framing and look at what Illumi and the rest of the Zoldyck family actually believe: they think the exact same way. Silva, Kikyo, and the family's entire upbringing operate on one shared rulebook—assassins do not trust outsiders, emotional attachments compromise survival, and the family's hierarchy decides what keeps an heir alive.

Illumi only absorbs all the heat because he is the one sent outside Kukuroo Mountain. Because the story puts him on screen whenever Killua steps out of line—at the Exam, in Yorknew, during the Election chase—readers mistake household policy for one man's private fixation. He is the visible enforcer carrying out an agreed family standard.

2. Outside the Gate Illumi Looks Crazy; Inside It, Killua Does

From our perspective as readers living in ordinary society, Killua is obviously the sane one. Wanting friends, walking away from forced murder, and rescuing Alluka look like healthy human rebellion.

Flip the camera to the Zoldyck estate, however, and the picture reverses completely. For generations, this syndicate has raised children through torture and poison tolerance to run a lethal family business. When their most gifted heir suddenly announces he doesn't want to kill anymore, starts bonding with outsiders, and insists on throwing himself into bad odds for friends, inside the Zoldyck worldview, Killua is the one acting completely irrational.

3. An Obedient Eldest Son Naturally Wants to Correct a Rebellious Brother

Then add the fact that Illumi is the firstborn son. Unlike Killua, Illumi swallowed every lesson the family handed down without a single complaint. He followed the doctrine literally—suppressing attachment, calculating odds, reshaping even his own body with needles to serve the trade.

To an eldest son who accepted every rule of the house as unquestionable reality, watching his younger brother spit on that training, rip out the survival needle, and run off with Nanika doesn't look like liberation—it looks like reckless defiance that will get Killua killed or ruin the family. Wanting to step in, scold him, and force the unruly brother back into line isn't random madness; inside his world, it feels like common sense and duty.
